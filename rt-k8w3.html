Den-en-chofu

田園調布

From the hotel: 48 min, leaves from Ueno Station, 2 changes, ¥440

Tokyo's classic money suburb, a planned garden suburb from the early 1900s. The streets fan out from the west side of the station, and some homes are big enough for tennis courts or pools, which is rare in Tokyo.

Walk out to Horai Park and back, 5 min each way.

Platinum Street

プラチナ通り, 白金台

Meguro Line from Den-en-chofu, one train, 11-17 min, ¥360

The gentle slope of Gaien-Nishi-dori from the Shirokanedai crossing up to Shirokane 6-chome. Shirokane is quiet, low-rise money: tree-lined streets and wealthy Japanese families.

Keep walking north on the same road to Hiroo: 25 min, 1.8 km.

Hiroo

広尾

On foot from Platinum Street

First choice for expat families. The National Azabu supermarket is the landmark for imported food, and Arisugawa Park is 3 min from the station. Condos by the station start around ¥150 million; bigger homes pass ¥1 billion.

Then walk through Azabu, the old embassy district, to Azabu-Juban: 14 min.

The rich areas

Minato, the richest of the 23 wards: Azabu, Hiroo, Shirokane, Akasaka, Roppongi, Aoyama.

Mansion suburbs: Den-en-chofu, Seijo (“Tokyo’s Beverly Hills”), and Shoto, a few minutes from Shibuya Station.

Trendy money: Daikanyama, Nakameguro, Ebisu.

Shopping money: Ginza (Friday) and Omotesando/Aoyama (Wednesday morning).

Japan’s priciest residential land is in Akasaka 1-chome: ¥5.9 million per m² in 2025, top for the eighth year running.
