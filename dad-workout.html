Strength Program

Powertec home gym, every other day, ~20 min per session.

Workout A

~20 min
Exercise Sets × Reps Rest Form cue
Goblet Squat (DB held at chest) 3 × 8–10 2 min Thighs to parallel. Chest tall, knees track over toes.
Chest-Supported Row (Powertec lever) 3 × 8–10 90 sec Chest pad firm. Pull elbows back, squeeze shoulder blades.
Lat Pulldown 2 × 10–12 75 sec Pull bar to upper chest. Slow on the way back up.
Dead Bug 2 × 10/side 30 sec Lower back stays pressed to floor. Slow, controlled.

Workout B

~20 min
Exercise Sets × Reps Rest Form cue
Goblet Squat (DB held at chest) 3 × 8–10 2 min Same as Day A. Squat is the most important lift here.
Flat Bench Press 2 × 8–10 90 sec Bar to mid-chest, full ROM. Feet planted, glutes tight.
Face Pulls (high cable rope) 3 × 12–15 60 sec Pull rope toward forehead. Elbows high, thumbs back.
Chin Tucks (lying on bench) 2 × 10 30 sec Lie on back. Gently tuck chin like making a double chin. Hold 3 sec.
How to alternate: A → rest day → B → rest day → A → rest day → B. Three to four sessions a week. Take an extra rest day anytime if a body part is still sore.

The rules

Effort
Stop each set when you have 2–3 reps left in the tank. No grinding to failure. The last rep should be hard but doable with good form.
Progression
When you hit the top of the rep range on every set with form intact, add the smallest increment of weight available next session.
Tempo
1–2 seconds lifting, 2–3 seconds lowering. The slow lower is where the muscle and tendon get stronger.
Warm-up
One light squat set at half your working weight, 8 reps. Skip static stretching — not needed before lifting.

4-week ramp-in

You haven't lifted in years. Going hard on session 1 is how you end up sidelined for two weeks. Build into it.

Weeks 1–2 Pick weights that feel like 50% effort. 4–5 reps in the tank. Movement quality only — learn the patterns.
Weeks 3–4 Working weight, 3–4 reps in the tank. Add a little weight when sets feel easy.
Week 5+ Full progression. 2–3 reps in the tank on every set, smallest-increment weight increases.

If pain flares

Some next-day muscle soreness is normal — not the same thing as joint or back pain. If back or neck pain spikes within 24 hours after a session:

  • Drop weight 10–15% on whichever exercise caused it.
  • Rebuild from there over the next 1–2 sessions.
  • If pain persists more than a few days, or you get numbness or tingling down arms or legs, see your doctor or a physical therapist before continuing.

Why these exercises specifically

Goblet squat instead of barbell or lever squat (for now)

The Powertec lever squat puts load on the shoulders and upper back, which can aggravate neck pain. Goblet squat keeps the load at chest level — no spinal compression. Same training effect on legs, glutes, and lower-back stability. After 4–6 weeks of pain-free squatting, you can switch to the lever station if you want.

Chest-supported row instead of bent-over row

Bending over with a heavy weight is a known back-pain trigger. The Powertec lever row done from a chest-supported position keeps the spine neutral and still hits the upper back hard.

Face pulls + chin tucks for the neck

Face pulls strengthen the muscles between the shoulder blades (mid trap, lower trap, rear deltoid). These muscles pull the shoulders back and the head over the spine instead of forward — which is exactly what counters the forward-head posture that drives most chronic neck pain. Chin tucks add a low-load, neck-specific strengthening piece. Multiple studies have shown progressive resistance training reduces chronic neck pain significantly.

Squat is the centerpiece for back pain

Squatting strengthens the glutes, hamstrings, and spinal erectors — the muscles that protect the lower back. Strong glutes in particular have been shown to reduce chronic low-back pain. This is why squat shows up every session, not just half the time.

Nutrition note

Get 25–30 grams of protein at each of 3–4 meals across the day. Whey or milk-based protein has the strongest evidence for boosting muscle gains alongside lifting. A scoop of whey in milk after the workout is fine; so is regular meat, eggs, fish, or Greek yogurt at meals.

  • Lai et al, 2023 — Dose-response RCT in older adults. Both intensity and volume drive strength and physical-function gains. DOI link
  • Ralston et al, 2017 — Meta-analysis on weekly set volume and strength. Medium volume (5–9 sets per exercise per week) outperforms low volume. DOI link

  • Farragher et al, 2024 — Resistance training alone is as effective as resistance training plus dedicated lumbar control retraining for chronic low-back pain. DOI link
