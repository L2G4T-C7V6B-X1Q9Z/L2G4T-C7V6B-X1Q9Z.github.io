The North Carolina Aviation Museum Is Weirdly Great for Toddlers

A warehouse full of vintage warplanes doesn't scream "toddler destination." But somehow it works.

By Katie Moorefield · March 4, 2026 · 5 min read

I'll be honest. When my husband suggested driving an hour and a half to look at old planes with a 2-year-old and a 4-year-old, I gave him the look. You know the one. The "are you trying to ruin a Saturday" look. But we'd already done every playground in a 20-mile radius, the NC Zoo was too big of a commitment for the day, and I was running out of ideas. So we drove to Asheboro.

And it was great. Genuinely, unexpectedly great.

What It Is

The North Carolina Aviation Museum is a large warehouse-style building on the edge of the Asheboro Regional Airport. It's not flashy. There's no gift shop full of overpriced trinkets, no interactive digital exhibits, no mascot walking around in a flight suit. It's basically a big metal building packed with vintage aircraft from World War II, Korea, Vietnam, and beyond. There are maybe 25 to 30 planes in various states of restoration, plus engines, propellers, uniforms, and other memorabilia lining the walls.

What makes it work for small kids is the vibe. It's low-key and uncrowded. The volunteers who run the place are retired aviation enthusiasts and military veterans, and they are shockingly patient with little kids. Nobody batted an eye when my 2-year-old tried to hug a landing gear strut.

Why Toddlers Love It

The planes are big and loud-looking and right there. Not behind glass, not across a velvet rope. They're just sitting in this open warehouse and your kids can walk right up to them. Some of the aircraft have open cockpits or doors, and the volunteers will actively invite kids to climb up and sit inside. My 4-year-old sat in the cockpit of a trainer plane and spent a solid 10 minutes pretending to fly it while making engine sounds that were, honestly, not far off from the real thing.

There's also a helicopter that they'll let kids sit in, and a handful of engines and propeller assemblies that are low enough to the ground for toddlers to touch and spin (slowly, with supervision). The whole place has a "please touch" energy that's the opposite of most museums. Nobody's hovering. Nobody's nervous. It's just old planes and enthusiastic old guys who want to tell you about them.

My 2-year-old mostly ran laps around the landing gear and pointed at things while yelling "BIG PLANE." Valid review.

The Logistics

Address: 2222-G Pilots Way, Asheboro, NC 27205
Distance from Durham: ~1 hour 30 minutes
Hours: Tue-Sat, 11am-5pm · Sun 1-5pm · Closed Monday
Cost: adults, kids (under 5 free)
Time to spend: 45 min to 1.5 hours depending on your kid's attention span

The drive is almost entirely on US-64, which is a pretty easy road. Parking is free and right in front of the building. There's a bathroom inside. No food on site, but there are a handful of fast-casual spots in Asheboro within a few minutes' drive. We hit up a Biscuitville on the way home and called it a win.

Stroller access is fine since it's all one level with wide aisles between the aircraft, though you'll probably want to let your kid out to roam. There's nothing to trip on or knock over. It's a warehouse. The floor is concrete. Bring that energy.

Pair It With the Zoo

Here's the real move: the North Carolina Zoo is literally 10 minutes down the road. If you're already driving to Asheboro, you might as well make a full day of it. We've done the combo twice now. Hit the aviation museum in the morning when it's fresh and your kids have energy, then drive over to the zoo for the afternoon. Or, if the zoo is the main event, save the museum for the last hour of the day as a cool-down activity. It works either way.

The zoo is a whole separate article (and a whole separate level of exhaustion), but the proximity makes this one of the better two-stop day trips you can put together from Durham.

Who Should Go

If your kid is in the phase where large vehicles are the most important thing in the world, this is your museum. Planes, helicopters, engines, propellers. It's all here and it's all accessible. The volunteers genuinely love kids and will go out of their way to make the visit memorable. My 4-year-old talked about "the plane museum" for two weeks straight afterward.

Is it worth the 90-minute drive on its own? Maybe, if your household is especially plane-obsessed. But paired with the zoo or a stop for lunch in Asheboro, it becomes a legitimately solid day trip. And at a head with kids under 5 free, the price is about as good as it gets for a museum experience anywhere.

The bottom line: Don't overthink it. It's cheap, it's chill, the volunteers are wonderful, and your toddler gets to sit in a cockpit. That's a good Saturday.

Information current as of March 2026. Hours may vary seasonally, so check before you go. If you've visited recently and something's changed, let us know.
