Radiology: training timeline and compensation

US data, 2026. Figures and sources listed at the end.

Definition

The specialty

A radiologist is a physician who interprets medical images: X-ray, CT, MRI, ultrasound, and nuclear medicine studies. The radiologist reviews the images, determines the diagnosis, and issues a written report to the physician who ordered the study.

Most of the work is performed at a multi-monitor workstation rather than at the bedside. Some radiologists also perform image-guided procedures, such as biopsies and drainages. Because the work does not require the patient to be present, a substantial share of it can be performed remotely: 25.6% of radiology job listings in 2026 were fully remote.

Total: six years of post-medical-school training at an average stipend of $76,018 per year, followed by a single-year increase of approximately 5.8x. Compensation in radiology is a step function, not a gradual progression. After partnership, income is driven by practice performance and hours worked rather than by seniority.

Automation

Artificial intelligence

In 2016 a widely reported prediction held that medical schools should stop training radiologists because image interpretation would be automated within five to ten years.

Ten years on, measured outcomes are the reverse:

  • 82.4% of radiology job postings in 2026 make no mention of AI tools, despite more than 873 FDA-cleared algorithms existing.
  • Radiology position counts reached an all-time high in the 2026 residency match, and there is a documented national shortage of radiologists.
  • Imaging volume is growing 3-4% per year while the radiologist workforce is approximately flat.

Two structural reasons are cited: legal responsibility for a diagnosis rests with a licensed physician, and no vendor has accepted that liability; and rising imaging volume absorbs efficiency gains rather than reducing headcount.

A 2026 survey of 401 medical students and residents found 2.5% considered AI "extremely influential" on specialty choice, while 33% of those interested in radiology reported feeling discouraged by it.

Risks

Documented downside factors

  • Medicare reimbursement. The 2027 physician fee schedule reduces the conversion factor by 1.68%, though the net estimated impact for diagnostic radiology is +2% after other adjustments. Long-term reimbursement pressure is the most frequently cited financial risk.
  • Private-equity acquisition. Roughly 10% of the radiology market. Documented outcomes for junior physicians after acquisition include compensation 20-25% below projection, increased workload, and turnover exceeding 50% of associate cohorts in reported cases.
  • Geography. The highest-paying markets are the least populated. Compensation and location preference are directly in tension.
