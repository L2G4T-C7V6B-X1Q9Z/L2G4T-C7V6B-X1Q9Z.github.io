Every "Pick Your Own" Farm Within an Hour of Raleigh, Ranked

We drove to all of them so you'd know which ones are worth the gas money and which ones are just a field with a cash box.

By David Lam · January 29, 2026 · 7 min read

The Triangle has an embarrassment of pick-your-own farms. Within an hour of Raleigh you can pick strawberries, blueberries, blackberries, apples, peaches, pumpkins, flowers, and at one ambitious operation near Benson, muscadine grapes. The problem isn't finding a farm. The problem is figuring out which one is actually going to be a good experience for your family versus which one is going to be a hot, crowded disappointment with a parking situation that makes you question your life choices.

Over the past two seasons, my daughters and I have visited every major u-pick operation within about an hour of Raleigh. We've picked in the rain. We've picked in 95-degree heat. We've waited in lines that made Disney look efficient. And we've found some absolute gems that nobody talks about. Here's the definitive ranking.

The Rankings

1. Herndon Hills Farm

Durham area · ~25 min from Raleigh

Herndon Hills is the farm that other farms should study. The picking fields are well-maintained, clearly marked, and never feel overcrowded because they actually manage capacity. You check in, grab a basket, and walk out to rows that still have fruit on them, which sounds obvious but isn't always the case at popular spots. Their strawberry season runs mid-April through late May, and the blueberries go June through July.

What puts Herndon Hills at number one is the overall experience. The staff are friendly without being overbearing. The checkout process is fast. The parking situation is organized. And the produce quality is consistently excellent. My daughters ate so many strawberries in the field last spring that they didn't want dinner, which is the highest compliment a 6-year-old can pay.

What to pick: Strawberries (Apr-May), Blueberries (Jun-Jul)
Cost: Market price by the pound
Tip: Go on a weekday morning. Saturday by 10am the parking lot is a nightmare.

2. Phillips Farms

Cary, NC · ~15 min from Raleigh

Phillips Farms wins on convenience alone. It's right there in Cary, no rural highway driving required, and the strawberry season operation they run is tight. Clean fields, fast check-in, and a small farm store with baked goods and local honey. The berries themselves are excellent, large and sweet with that actual strawberry flavor that grocery store berries forgot about years ago.

The downside is that everyone in Wake County knows about Phillips Farms, so peak weekends are packed. They've started implementing timed entry on Saturdays, which helps, but you still want to aim early. Their season is typically mid-April through mid-May, and they post daily updates on whether fields are open. Check before you go.

What to pick: Strawberries (mid-Apr to mid-May)
Cost: ~$4-5/lb
Tip: Their strawberry lemonade at the farm store is unreasonably good. Budget an extra $5 for drinks.

3. Vollmer Farm

Bunn, NC · ~40 min from Raleigh

Vollmer Farm is the all-rounder. They do strawberries in spring, sunflowers in summer, and a massive fall festival with a corn maze, pumpkin patch, and hayrides that draws half of Franklin County. The u-pick experience is solid across the board, but where Vollmer really shines is the fall season. Their corn maze is legitimately challenging (we got lost for 25 minutes last October, which my daughters thought was hilarious and I found stressful), and the pumpkin selection is huge.

The drive to Bunn takes about 40 minutes from Raleigh, mostly on US-401, and it feels like you're leaving civilization for a bit. That's part of the charm. Vollmer is a big operation, so it can handle crowds, but fall weekends still get intense. Their sunflower fields in July and August are also gorgeous and very popular with the Instagram crowd.

What to pick: Strawberries (spring), Sunflowers (Jul-Aug), Pumpkins (fall)
Cost: Varies by season and activity
Tip: The fall festival is worth the hype, but go on a Friday evening if possible. Weekend afternoons are chaos.

4. Hillcrest Farms

Goldston, NC · ~55 min from Raleigh

Hillcrest is the sleeper pick on this list. It's further out than most people want to drive for berries, but that's exactly why it's great. When Herndon Hills and Phillips are slammed on a Saturday morning, Hillcrest is calm. You park easily, walk right into the fields, and pick at a pace that doesn't feel like a competitive sport.

The farm itself is beautiful. Rolling hills, well-kept rows, and a sense of quiet that you just don't get at the more popular places. They grow strawberries, blueberries, and blackberries across a long season (April through August if the weather cooperates). The blackberries in particular are outstanding. Huge, sweet, and so abundant that you fill a bucket before you realize it.

The trade-off is the drive. Goldston is southwest of Pittsboro on winding two-lane roads, and there's nothing else out there to pair it with. This is a dedicated trip. But if you want the most relaxed, pastoral u-pick experience in the region, Hillcrest is it.

What to pick: Strawberries (Apr-May), Blueberries (Jun-Jul), Blackberries (Jul-Aug)
Cost: Market price by the pound
Tip: Bring sunscreen and water. There's minimal shade in the picking fields.

5. Jones Family Farms

Benson, NC · ~45 min from Raleigh

Jones Family Farms is a solid operation that does a lot of things well without being the absolute best at any one thing. Their strawberry fields are reliable, their fall activities are fun, and they've recently expanded into sunflower picking, which is a nice addition. The farm has a friendly, unpretentious vibe that makes it easy to spend a morning there.

What's interesting about Jones is their muscadine grapes, which you can pick in late August and September. Not many farms in the area offer u-pick muscadines, and if you've never had a fresh muscadine straight off the vine, you're missing out. They're sweet, musky, and distinctly Southern. My daughters were skeptical but converted after about three grapes each.

The farm is south of Raleigh on the way toward Benson, so it's convenient if you're coming from south Raleigh or Clayton. From north Raleigh or Durham, it's a longer haul and may not be worth it over closer options unless you're specifically after muscadines.

What to pick: Strawberries (spring), Sunflowers (summer), Muscadine Grapes (Aug-Sep), Pumpkins (fall)
Cost: Varies by crop
Tip: The muscadine season is short. Follow them on Facebook for the exact window.

Seasonal Quick Reference

Strawberries: Mid-April through late May
Blueberries: June through July
Blackberries: July through August
Sunflowers: July through August
Muscadines: Late August through September
Apples: September through October
Pumpkins: Late September through October

General Tips for U-Pick With Kids

  • Go early. Before 10am on weekends, before noon on weekdays. The fields get hot, the crowds get thick, and the best fruit gets picked by mid-morning on busy days.
  • Bring your own water. Some farms have water stations, many don't. Dehydrated kids are cranky kids.
  • Wear closed-toe shoes. The fields are dirt, mud, or mulch. Sandals are a mistake you'll make once.
  • Budget for eating in the field. Your kids will eat berries. You will eat berries. Everyone eats berries. It's fine, farms expect it, just don't be egregious about it.
  • Check social media before driving. Almost every farm posts daily updates on whether fields are open and what's ripe. A 40-minute drive to find out the strawberries are done is a bad morning.

Rankings reflect our personal experience over the 2024 and 2025 seasons. Availability, pricing, and hours change seasonally. Always verify before making the trip.
